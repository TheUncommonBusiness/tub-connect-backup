Plain Text
Okay so here is the thing that drives me crazy. I send like three to five follow up emails every single day after meetings, and by the evening I cannot remember who said they would do what. So I sit there for about thirty minutes rebuilding it from my notes, and half the time I am guessing. Everybody is waiting on me for the decisions and who owns what and by when.

What I want is to paste in a transcript and get back the follow up email, written the way I would send it. And I am a little obsessed with my template, it has to look exactly the same every time so my team knows where to look.

So the subject line has the project name and then says decisions, owners, and what I need by Friday. Then "Hi team," and one line that says this is a quick recap so we are all working off the same page. Then what we decided, each decision with one line of context. Then what is still open, so the question, who owns getting us an answer, and by when. Then the action items, each one as the owner, the specific thing they are delivering, and the date. Then what I need from each person this week, just the one thing next to each name. Then the risks I am watching, with the trigger that would make me escalate.

And at the bottom I always say that if anything here does not match your understanding, push back today, because silence means agreement and I will move on it Monday. Then thanks.

A few rules. Same format every time, no extra sections, and do not make it wordy. Plain English, like a sixth grader could read it. If something is unclear, flag it with needs confirmation in brackets. If nobody got named for a task, put owner needed in brackets, please do not guess. If there are no risks, just say none right now. And no long summary of the meeting, I was in the meeting.

Before you start, ask me any clarifying questions you need.
